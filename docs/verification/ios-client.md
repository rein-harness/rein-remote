# Verifying the iOS and iPadOS client

The app is `clients/ios` (SwiftUI, its Xcode project generated by XcodeGen) on the Rust core
(`crates/rr-ffi`, rr-client behind a C ABI, built into `ReinRemoteCore.xcframework`). What the
simulator could check was checked there (below, with the numbers); a real iPhone and iPad are checked
by hand with the steps after that.

## Building

On a Mac with Xcode (16 or later) and Rust:

```sh
rustup target add aarch64-apple-ios aarch64-apple-ios-sim   # x86_64-apple-ios too on an Intel Mac
brew install xcodegen
cd clients/ios
./build-core.sh            # the core for a device and this Mac's simulator (./build-core.sh sim: the simulator only)
xcodegen                   # ReinRemote.xcodeproj, from project.yml
open ReinRemote.xcodeproj
```

Run `./build-core.sh` again after changing anything in `crates/`; `xcodegen` again after adding or
removing a Swift file.

## What the simulator checks (and CI, on every push)

`rein-remote serve --test-pattern 1280x720` on the Mac, the simulator connecting to it (it shares
the Mac's network and its clock):

```sh
rein-remote serve --test-pattern 1280x720 --listen 127.0.0.1:7979 --token t --mdns off &
cd clients/ios
TEST_RUNNER_RR_URL='http://127.0.0.1:7979/?token=t' xcodebuild test -project ReinRemote.xcodeproj \
  -scheme ReinRemote -destination "id=$(./simulator.sh)" -derivedDataPath DerivedData
```

- **Units**: Annex B to VideoToolbox's format, the touch gestures (tap, swipe, hold and drag,
  two-finger tap), key names and HID usages, the on-screen keyboard's text and keys, a gamepad's
  state in the protocol's layout, the barcode reader, the QR code's link.
- **The whole way** (`StreamTests`): connects through the core, decodes with VideoToolbox, sends the
  pointer to three places and finds the test pattern's red cross at each in the decoded frames (the
  input reached the server and came back in the video), then measures for 8 s. It prints a
  `selftest:` line.
- **A tap through UIKit** (`UITests`): the app launched with `-RRConnect URL`, a tap on the stream
  goes through UIKit's touches and the gestures as a click; the cross shows where it landed.
- `TEST_RUNNER_RR_PAIR_URL=http://<this Mac's address>:7979/ TEST_RUNNER_RR_PAIR_CODE=<code>` with a
  server listening on `0.0.0.0` (no `--token`: it prints a pairing code): pairs with the code, then
  signs in again with its key and nothing typed. `TEST_RUNNER_RR_EXPECT_SERVER=<its name>`: the
  server list finds it by Bonjour, and (once paired) a tap on it resolves it and signs in.
- `TEST_RUNNER_RR_CODEC=HEVC` against `serve --codec h265`; `TEST_RUNNER_RR_WEBTRANSPORT=off` for
  the WebSocket; `TEST_RUNNER_RR_NET=1`: a real certificate checks out (the core brings Mozilla's
  roots on iOS, where the system's can't be read).

The same self-test runs from the app itself, for a device: launch arguments `-RRConnect URL
-RRSelfTest YES` (`-RRSeconds N`, `-RRHEVC YES`, `-RRAudio NO`, `-RRWebTransport NO`); the line shows
on the screen and in the device's log.

Measured on one Mac (Apple silicon, 8 GB, other work running), the iOS 26.5 simulator, the test
pattern at 1280×720 and 60 fps, 8–10 s each:

| Device, path | fps | Glass to glass p50 / p95 | Decode p50 / p95 | Input to cross seen | Sound against picture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone 17, H.264, WebTransport datagrams | 60.0 | 9 ms / 15 ms | 1.5 ms / 4.4 ms | 51–54 ms | 15–22 ms |
| iPhone 17, HEVC (`--codec h265`), datagrams | 60.0 | 9 ms / 17 ms | 1.5 ms / 3.5 ms | 53 ms | 19 ms |
| iPhone 17, H.264, WebSocket | 60.0 | 17–18 ms / 22–40 ms | 2–3 ms / 5–22 ms | 53–55 ms | 13–18 ms |
| iPad Pro 11-inch (M5), H.264, datagrams | 45 | 20 ms / 101 ms | 1.4 ms / 10 ms | 51–54 ms | 14 ms |

Glass to glass is the test pattern's barcode (its capture time) read from each decoded frame as it
goes to the display layer, against the same clock: capture, encode, the network, decode. The
display's next refresh adds up to one more frame. "Input to cross seen" is from sending a pointer
move to the first decoded frame with the cross there: the input's way to the server, a frame's
capture, and that frame's way back. Sound against picture: each beep of the test tone as scheduled
to be heard, against its flash as decoded (positive: the sound later). The iPad Pro simulator says
its display runs at 120 Hz, so the server paced to 120 frames a second on a busy Mac, fell behind
and lowered the picture to 640×360 for a while (the barcode can't be read at that size): a number
for this Mac, not for an iPad.

**The simulator isn't a phone**: it decodes on the Mac (in software, not an iPhone's hardware
decoder), shares its network (no Wi-Fi in between) and its clock (glass to glass needs no clock
sync there), and has no camera, gamepads, pointer or real touch. The numbers above are for the
pipeline, not for a device.

## On a real iPhone and iPad

What the simulator can't check: the hardware decoder, Wi-Fi, real touch, the on-screen and hardware
keyboards, a trackpad or mouse on iPad, a gamepad, the speaker, the camera (QR pairing), and iOS's
local network permission.

### Signing (a free Apple ID works)

1. Xcode > Settings > Accounts: add your Apple ID (a free one is enough; apps it signs run for 7
   days, then sign them again).
2. In `clients/ios/project.yml`, set `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM` to your team ID (Xcode shows it under the
   account; for a free account it's "Your Name (Personal Team)"'s ID) and change
   `PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER` to one of your own (a free account can't use `dev.reinharness.remote`):
   for instance `com.yourname.reinremote`. Run `xcodegen`. (Or pick the team and a bundle ID in
   Xcode's Signing & Capabilities tab after each `xcodegen`.)
3. Connect the device by cable, trust the Mac on it, and turn on Developer Mode (Settings > Privacy &
   Security > Developer Mode; it restarts). `./build-core.sh` (with the device slice), then in Xcode
   pick the device and Run.
4. The first time: Settings > General > VPN & Device Management > your Apple ID > Trust.

### The server

On a Mac or Linux machine on the same Wi-Fi:

```sh
rein-remote serve --test-pattern 1280x720 --listen 0.0.0.0:7979     # for the numbers
rein-remote serve --window TextEdit --listen 0.0.0.0:7979           # for input on a real app (macOS)
rein-remote serve --listen 0.0.0.0:7979                             # the screen (Linux: $DISPLAY)
```

It prints its links, a pairing code and a QR code, and says it's "on this network as NAME".

### What to check

Write down the device, its iOS version, the server's machine and how they're connected, and for each
item what happened.

1. **Finding it.** Open the app: iOS asks to find devices on the local network: allow. The server
   shows under "On this network" by its name within a few seconds. (Denied: nothing shows; Settings >
   Privacy & Security > Local Network turns it back on.)
2. **Pairing by code.** Tap it, enter the code the server printed, Connect. The stream starts; the
   server prints `paired with <the device's name>`. Close it (×), tap the server again: it connects
   with no code (signed in with the key kept in the app's container).
3. **Pairing by QR code.** Restart the server for a new code; on another device (or after deleting
   and reinstalling the app), + > Scan its QR code, point the camera at the QR code in the terminal.
   It connects without typing.
4. **A link.** + > paste a link the server printed (`http://…/?token=…`); with Tailscale and its
   HTTPS on, the `https://` tailnet link too (a real certificate). Through a relay
   (`serve --relay …`): its `…/?room=…#k=…` link; the server prints whether a direct path was
   punched.
5. **Numbers** (test pattern): the speedometer button shows the decoder (`H.264 1280×720 (hardware)`
   on a device), fps, the path, the round trip and, when the device's clock agrees with the server's
   (both on network time; a few ms apart at most), glass to glass. Or run the self-test: in Xcode,
   Product > Scheme > Edit Scheme > Run > Arguments: `-RRConnect http://<server>:7979/?token=…
   -RRSelfTest YES`; the `selftest:` line comes on the screen. Expect 60 fps and the cross seen at
   all three points (`PASS`); write down the line. With `serve --codec h265` on a Mac, the decoder
   says `HEVC … (hardware)`.
6. **Touch** (on a real app, e.g. TextEdit or a terminal): a tap clicks (the caret moves there); a
   swipe scrolls what's under the finger, the content following it; hold for half a second (a light
   tap of the haptics), then move: drags (selects text); a two-finger tap right-clicks (a context
   menu, between the fingers).
7. **The on-screen keyboard**: the keyboard button; type words, Return, delete: they arrive as typed
   (autocorrect off), Return as Enter, delete as Backspace; emoji and accents as text.
8. **A hardware keyboard** (a Magic Keyboard, a Smart Keyboard on iPad, or Bluetooth): letters,
   Shift for capitals, arrows, Escape, Tab, Return, Backspace, F-keys, Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V in a terminal.
   (iOS keeps some chords for itself: Cmd+Tab, Cmd+Space, the Globe key's.)
9. **The pointer on iPad** (a Magic Trackpad, a mouse, or a Magic Keyboard's trackpad): moving
   without clicking moves the remote pointer (drawn by the app, in the server's shape: the arrow,
   the I-beam over text); a click clicks; a two-finger click (or a mouse's right button) right-clicks;
   two fingers on the trackpad (or the wheel) scroll; click and drag selects.
10. **A gamepad** (an Xbox, PlayStation or MFi controller, paired in Settings > Bluetooth): with the
    speedometer on, a line `gamepads: 1 · buttons 0x… · left x,y` changes as buttons and sticks
    move (A is 0x1, the d-pad right 0x8000; up on the left stick is negative). No server injects
    gamepads yet, so it says `not sent: the server takes no gamepads`; a server that does takes them
    in the W3C standard layout (rr-proto's `Input::Gamepad`).
11. **Sound**: with the test pattern, a beep each second with the white flash in the top left, heard
    from the speaker together with the flash (the overlay counts packets played and dropped; the
    self-test's `av_sync_ms_p50`, on a device whose clock agrees, says how far apart). With a real
    machine, play something there: it plays here. The mute switch doesn't silence it (it's playback).
12. **Rotation and iPad multitasking**: turn the device, use Split View or Stage Manager: the
    picture stays fitted and taps still land where they're aimed.
13. **Going away**: lock the device or leave the app for a minute and come back; switch Wi-Fi off and
    on: the session ends with a reason on the screen; × and a tap on the server reconnect.
